Theoretical
      The study is anchored on the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), Task-Technology Fit (TTF), and DeLone and McLean Information Systems Success Model, which provide theoretical foundations for developing and evaluating the FieldTrack system. TAM explains how perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use influence the acceptance and willingness of the intended users to use the system. TTF supports the study by determining whether the system’s features are appropriate for the tasks involved in monitoring Field Study and Practicum students, such as attendance monitoring, requirements submission, activity recording, reminders, progress tracking, practicum or internship opportunities. The DeLone and McLean Information Systems Success Model further supports the study by assessing the system’s success through factors such as system quality, information quality, system use, user satisfaction, and net benefits. Together, these theories and models provide a foundation for evaluating the FieldTrack system in terms of user acceptance, task suitability, system quality, user satisfaction, and overall effectiveness as a digital monitoring solution.

Review Literature
Setyadi and Mardhiyyah (2025) developed an Android-Based Information System for Monitoring and Evaluation of Industrial Internship Activities at SMK N 2 Temanggung, Indonesia, to address the challenges of manually monitoring and evaluating students during industrial work practice. The study developed an Android-based system that enables students, teachers, and industry partners to use a mobile platform, while administrators manage information through a web-based system. The system integrates attendance, daily activity reports, and performance assessments into one platform, allowing supervisors to monitor students' activities remotely and in real time. The researchers found that integrating these functions into a single system improved administrative efficiency and simplified the monitoring and management of industrial work practice activities. The study is relevant to FieldTrack: A Digital Monitoring System for Field Study and Practicum Students at Norzagaray College, because both systems aim to replace manual monitoring with digital tools for recording activities, tracking student progress, and managing evaluations. However, Setyadi and Mardhiyyah (2025) focused on industrial work practice for vocational high school students in Indonesia, while FieldTrack is designed for Field Study and Practicum students at Norzagaray College and includes additional functions such as location tracking, notifications and reminders, status monitoring, and user account management. Therefore, the current study addresses a contextual gap by adapting an integrated digital monitoring approach to the specific academic and supervision requirements of Field Study and Practicum students in a Philippine college setting. 

	Nurfaizi and Hindarto (2023) developed the Web-Based Student Internship Attendance Application System at Universitas Nasional, Indonesia, to address the inefficiencies and inaccuracies associated with manually recording student internship attendance. The study used the Waterfall methodology to develop a web-based system that allows students to record daily attendance using mobile devices or computers and submit reports about their daily assignments and internship experiences, while lecturers, supervisors, and administrators can access attendance information for monitoring and evaluation. The system uses automated data capture and validation, real-time updates, structured attendance records, and automatic reporting to improve the efficiency, accuracy, accessibility, and transparency of attendance management. The researchers reported that the system reduced the time and effort required for manual attendance recording, minimized errors, provided real-time access to attendance information, and supported faster generation of attendance reports. The study is relevant to FieldTrack: A Digital Monitoring System for Field Study and Practicum Students at Norzagaray College, because both systems use web-based technology to digitally record student activities and attendance, monitor student participation, and assist supervisors in managing field-based academic requirements. However, Nurfaizi and Hindarto (2023) primarily focused on internship attendance monitoring and reporting, while FieldTrack is designed specifically for Field Study and Practicum students at Norzagaray College and provides broader functions such as student activity recording, progress tracking, location tracking, evaluation management, notifications and reminders, status monitoring, and user account management. Therefore, the current study addresses a functional and contextual gap by expanding digital attendance monitoring into a more comprehensive system for monitoring, evaluating, and managing the overall Field Study and Practicum experience in a Philippine college setting. 

Ahmed et al. (2026) developed A Digital Solution for Student Internship: Tracking and Enhancing Student’s Experience, an electronic logbook system for the Students Industrial Work Experience Scheme (SIWES) in Nigeria, to address problems associated with paper-based internship documentation, including record loss, poor supervision, delayed evaluation, and administrative burden. The researchers developed a web-based system that allows students to record daily or weekly activities online, supervisors to monitor and evaluate students remotely, and administrators to manage placements and generate reports. The system was implemented using web technologies and included role-based interfaces, log management, evaluation and reporting modules, placement management, notifications, and secure user authentication. The study found that the electronic logbook improved transparency, accessibility, data reliability, supervision efficiency, and performance monitoring while reducing paperwork and supporting accountability. The study is relevant to FieldTrack: A Digital Monitoring System for Field Study and Practicum Students at Norzagaray College, because both systems use digital technology to improve student activity recording, progress monitoring, supervision, evaluation, and academic management. However, Ahmed et al. (2026) focused on the SIWES internship framework in Nigeria, while FieldTrack 	is specifically designed for Field Study and Practicum students at Norzagaray College and incorporates student activity recording, progress tracking, location tracking, evaluation management, notifications and reminders, status monitoring, and user account management. Therefore, the current study addresses a contextual and functional gap by adapting digital internship monitoring to the specific academic requirements and supervision needs of Field Study and Practicum students in a Philippine college setting. 


Related Technologies
     Related technologies play an important role in the development of the proposed web-based and mobile-based Digital Monitoring System for Field Study/Internship Students. These technologies provide the necessary components for creating the system’s interface, processing information, storing records, and supporting communication between users and the system. The appropriate use of these technologies helps ensure that the system can efficiently support student monitoring, activity recording, progress tracking, reminder, evaluation, and information management.





HTML5, CSS, and JavaScript are used to develop the structure, appearance, and interactive functions of the system’s web interface. HTML5 organizes web page content and system elements, while CSS provides the design, layout, and responsive interface needed for access across different devices. JavaScript supports client-side behavior and interactive features, allowing users to perform actions such as submitting requirements, viewing records, and interacting with monitoring functions.





PHP and MySQL are used for the back-end processing and database management of the proposed system. PHP handles server-side processing, system logic, user requests, data validation, and API development, allowing the system to process information between users and its database. MySQL stores and manages student profiles, attendance, requirements, logbooks, and monitoring records, making information available for authorized users when needed.






Leaflet.js and OpenStreetMap support the system’s mapping and location-based functions by providing map display, GPS location tracking, and geofencing features. Flutter and Dart are used to develop the mobile application for students, including attendance recording, location capture, and submission of requirements. Artificial Intelligence (AI) through the OpenAI API supports automated validation of student requirements and internship matching or recommendations, while web technologies make the system accessible through web browsers for students, advisers, and administrators.
