FISHBONE DIAGRAM
The Fishbone Diagram is used to identify and organize the possible causes of problems encountered in the current monitoring and management process. It helps the researchers determine the root causes of the identified issues and serves as a basis for developing the proposed system.
Figure 6. Fishbone Diagram (Cause-and-Effect or Ishikawa Diagram)

The Figure 6 Fishbone Diagram (Cause-and-Effect or Ishikawa Diagram) illustrates the root causes contributing to the central problem of inefficient manual monitoring and management of Field Study and Practicum Students. The identified causes are categorized into six main operational areas: Requirements Verification, Attendance Monitoring, Submission Tracking, Progress Monitoring, Record Management, and Information Coordination.
Regarding Requirements Verification, the process is affected by manual document submission, delayed verification, and the absence of standardized verification procedures. These challenges may make it difficult for supervising instructors or advisers to efficiently check and confirm the completeness and validity of student requirements. In terms of Attendance Monitoring, handwritten attendance, late record checking, and incomplete time records may result in difficulties in maintaining accurate and updated attendance information.
For Submission Tracking, the absence of a centralized submission tracker, late identification of missing files, and inconsistent submissions can make it difficult to monitor the status of student requirements. Similarly, Progress Monitoring is affected by manual progress tracking, manual hour tracking, and the lack of real-time completion tracking, which may delay the monitoring of students' progress and completion status.
In terms of Record Management, paper-based records, slow record retrieval, and scattered student files can make the management and retrieval of important information inefficient. Lastly, Information Coordination is affected by separate communication channels, delayed updates, and the lack of shared access to information, which may result in communication gaps among students, advisers, and administrators.
These identified causes serve as the basis for the development of FieldTrack: A Digital Monitoring System for Field Study and Practicum Students at Norzagaray College, Bulacan. The proposed system aims to address these challenges by providing a centralized and organized platform for monitoring attendance, verifying requirements, tracking submissions and student progress, managing records, and improving information coordination.

Project Design 
The project is designed as a web-based and mobile-based digital platform that centralizes the management and monitoring of Field Study and Practicum activities. It will support student information management, attendance monitoring, requirements tracking and verification, activity recording, progress and status monitoring, evaluation management, notifications and reminders, and reports and records management.
The system will allow students to manage their information, attendance, requirements, and activities, while advisers and administrators can monitor student progress, verify requirements, conduct evaluations, and manage records. Notifications and reminders will help users stay informed about deadlines, activities, evaluations, and incomplete requirements.
The system will also include a Practicum/Internship Opportunity Matching feature that will assist students in finding potential companies or organizations for their required practicum or internship. It will match relevant student information, such as academic program, skills, interests, preferred location, and qualifications, with available opportunities. The system will provide recommended opportunities that students can review and select, while advisers or administrators can review and monitor the placement process.

Software Development Methodologies
        	The researchers adopted the Agile Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) to guide the systematic and iterative development of RETRASYS through planning, designing, developing, testing, and incorporating feedback for continuous improvement.










Figure 18. Agile Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC)

The process is compared to following phases:
Requirements Gathering Phases - In this phase, the research team conducted meetings
to identify system requirements, define key features, and establish the overall plan for the
FieldTrack system. This includes determining how the system will monitor student
activities, location, and performance.
Design Phase - During the design phase, the system architecture, data structures, and
flowcharts were developed. These designs ensure that the system meets the specific needs
of both students and administrators.
Development Phase - In this phase, the actual system was built. The researchers
developed both the front-end interface and the back-end database, incorporating features

Deployment Phase - After successful testing, the system was deployed for actual use. It
was implemented to monitor and manage field study activities in real-world settings.
Review and Maintenance Phase - In the final phase, the system is continuously
monitored and updated. The research team performs regular improvements, resolves
technical issues, and ensures that the system remains reliable, secure, and efficient over
time.
Research Design
The researchers used a quantitative-developmental research design in developing FieldTrack. The developmental approach helped the researchers plan, create, test, and improve the system, while the quantitative approach was used to gather and analyze numerical data from the system evaluation. Before the system was developed, an initial interview was conducted with selected respondents and concerned personnel to understand the existing problems, monitoring procedures, and system requirements that guided the development of FieldTrack
.
Requirement Analysis
The requirement analysis was conducted to identify the needs of the proposed FieldTrack system. It covered the functions, usability, security, and other requirements needed by field study and practicum students, supervising instructors/advisers, and administrators. These requirements were gathered from the current practices in attendance, requirements, progress, activities, reminders  and evaluation and were used as a guide in developing the system.
The functional requirements describe the main features of FieldTrack. The system shall allow users to log in and access features based on their assigned roles. It shall record and monitor student attendance, activities, submitted requirements, and progress. Students shall be able to submit requirements, reminders and activity reports, while supervising instructors/advisers shall be able to review, verify, and update student records. The system shall also provide evaluations, notifications and reminders, status monitoring, and record management. These features are designed to make the monitoring of field study and practicum students more organized and easier to manage.
The non-functional requirements focus on the overall quality of FieldTrack. The system shall be evaluated using ISO/IEC 25010 based on the selected software quality characteristics such as functional suitability, reliability, and security. It should perform its functions properly, work consistently, and keep user information protected. The system shall also be assessed using ISO 9241-11:2018 in terms of effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction. The interface should be simple enough for the intended users to perform their tasks correctly and without unnecessary difficulty. These requirements help ensure that the proposed system is reliable, secure, useful, and easy to use.

Sampling Technique
The study will use Stratified Random Sampling as the sampling technique to select the respondents. This technique is appropriate because the population consists of students from different programs and sections, including the Bachelor of Elementary Education (BEED), Bachelor of Secondary Education (BSED), and Associate in Computer Technology (ACT). The respondents will first be grouped according to their respective programs and sections, after which participants will be randomly selected from each group to ensure that all groups are properly represented in the study. This technique will help obtain a more balanced and representative sample for the evaluation of the proposed FieldTrack system.
